## Introduction

Software Engineering has evolved significantly over the past few decades, driven by technological advancements and the increasing complexity of software systems. As organizations strive to enhance productivity, improve software quality, and reduce time-to-market, the adoption of innovative methodologies and tools has become paramount. This paper investigates the key factors driving the adoption of Software Engineering practices, particularly in light of recent advancements in Artificial Intelligence (AI), such as Large Language Models (LLMs). It also addresses the limitations that organizations face in this transition, especially concerning legacy systems. By analyzing recent research and industry trends, this paper aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of the current landscape of Software Engineering and its future trajectory.

## Why This Matters

Understanding the factors that drive the adoption of Software Engineering practices is crucial for organizations aiming to remain competitive in a rapidly evolving technological landscape. The integration of AI tools, particularly LLMs, has the potential to revolutionize various aspects of software development, from requirements gathering to maintenance. However, the resistance encountered due to legacy systems poses significant challenges. By exploring these dynamics, this paper highlights the importance of strategic planning and investment in new technologies, ensuring that organizations can leverage the benefits of modern Software Engineering practices while mitigating associated risks.

## Background

Software Engineering as a discipline has undergone substantial evolution since its inception in the 1960s. Initially focused on the development of software systems through structured methodologies, the field has expanded to include agile practices, DevOps, and now, AI-driven approaches. The introduction of LLMs marks a significant shift, enabling more sophisticated interactions between humans and machines. Ontology learning, a critical aspect of knowledge representation in software systems, has seen enhanced capabilities through LLMs, allowing for more efficient data organization and retrieval. However, the integration of these advanced technologies is not without its challenges, particularly when considering the entrenched nature of legacy systems that many organizations still rely on.

## Research Question

What are the key factors driving Software Engineering adoption and what limitations remain?

## Technical Analysis

The technical landscape of Software Engineering is increasingly influenced by the integration of AI technologies, particularly LLMs. These models have demonstrated significant potential in enhancing various software engineering processes, notably ontology learning. Ontology learning involves the automatic extraction of concepts and relationships from data, which is essential for knowledge representation in software systems. Research indicates that LLMs can significantly improve the efficiency and accuracy of this process. For instance, the study 'When Does Bigger Help? A Controlled Study of LLM Scale for Ontology Learning' (reliability: 100%) provides empirical evidence that larger models yield better performance in ontology learning tasks, suggesting that the scale of LLMs directly correlates with their effectiveness in this domain.

Post-training of LLMs can be conceptualized as a form of brownfield maintenance within software engineering. This perspective is explored in the paper 'LLM Post-Training as Brownfield Maintenance: An Industrial Perspective on Dataware Engineering' (reliability: 100%). Here, the authors argue that just as brownfield maintenance involves updating and improving existing systems, post-training LLMs allows organizations to adapt these models to their specific contexts and datasets. This adaptability is crucial as it enables organizations to leverage existing knowledge while integrating new capabilities, thereby enhancing the overall software development lifecycle.

The Aider Desk tool exemplifies a practical application of AI in software engineering. This tool streamlines the integration of AI into software engineering tasks, facilitating a more efficient workflow for developers. According to its documentation (reliability: 95%), Aider Desk provides features that assist in code generation, debugging, and documentation, thereby reducing the cognitive load on developers and allowing them to focus on more complex problem-solving tasks. The tool's design reflects a growing trend towards AI-assisted software engineering, where developers increasingly rely on AI to augment their capabilities.

Despite the promising advancements, the adoption of AI tools in software engineering is not without challenges. A significant barrier to adoption is the resistance stemming from legacy systems. Many organizations continue to operate on outdated software architectures that are not easily compatible with modern AI tools. This resistance is highlighted in the interpretation that while the adoption of AI tools is growing, the need to maintain compatibility with legacy systems hampers the transition to AI-driven methodologies. Organizations must navigate these complexities, balancing the integration of new technologies with the realities of their existing infrastructure.

In summary, the technical analysis reveals that while LLMs and AI tools present significant opportunities for enhancing software engineering practices, the integration of these technologies is complicated by the presence of legacy systems and the need for organizations to adapt their existing processes. The evolution of Software Engineering is thus characterized by a dual focus on innovation and the challenges posed by entrenched practices.

## Limitations

Despite the promising advancements in Software Engineering driven by AI, several limitations and challenges persist. One significant knowledge gap is the long-term performance implications of LLMs in dynamic software environments. While current research demonstrates their effectiveness in specific tasks, the sustainability of these benefits over time remains uncertain. Additionally, there is limited research on the specific challenges organizations face when integrating AI tools with legacy systems. This gap highlights the need for further investigation into the compatibility issues and transition strategies that organizations must navigate. Furthermore, the effectiveness of AI tools in various software engineering domains beyond ontology learning is still an open question, necessitating broader research to fully understand their potential applications.
